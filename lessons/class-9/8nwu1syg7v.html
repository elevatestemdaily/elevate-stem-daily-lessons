Today's Science Mystery

Could we ever travel to another star?

10 min readDaily STEMClass 9
Today's Mission

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain the challenges of interstellar travel, calculate travel times, and predict how long a journey to Proxima Centauri at 10% light speed would take.

1. The mystery

Proxima Centauri is the nearest star to our Sun — 4.24 light-years away. That sounds manageable. But a light-year is 9.46 trillion km. Voyager 1, launched in 1977 and now the farthest human-made object (~23 billion km out), would take 73,000 years to reach Proxima Centauri. Could we ever do better?

2. The science behind it

The distance problem: 1 light-year = 9.461 × 10¹² km. Proxima Centauri: 4.24 ly = 4.01 × 10¹³ km. Voyager 1 speed: 17 km/s = 0.00006c. Time to Proxima: 4.01×10¹³ / 17 = 2.36×10¹² seconds ≈ 74,800 years.

At 10% of light speed (c = 3×10⁸ m/s): v = 0.1c = 30,000 km/s. Time = 4.24 ly / 0.1c = 42.4 years.

The energy problem: Accelerating a 10,000 kg spacecraft to 0.1c requires kinetic energy: KE ≈ ½mv² = ½ × 10⁴ × (3×10⁷)² = 4.5 × 10¹⁸ J This equals the entire world's energy production for ~10 years.

Proposed approaches: - Nuclear pulse propulsion (Project Orion): nuclear explosions behind the ship. Could reach 0.033c. - Laser sail (Breakthrough Starshot): tiny probe, giant laser array pushes a light sail. Target: 0.2c. Could reach Proxima in ~20 years — but a gram-scale probe. - Antimatter drive (theoretical): matter-antimatter annihilation → pure energy. Most energy-dense fuel possible. Technology far from real.

Relativistic effects at high speeds (> ~0.1c): Time dilation — onboard clocks run slower. At 0.9c, 42.4 year journey → only ~18 years experienced by travellers.

3. Build the mental model

Picture the Earth-to-Moon distance (384,000 km) as a single step — roughly 30 cm on a scale model. At that same scale, Proxima Centauri is 160 km away (roughly the straight-line distance from Delhi to Chandigarh). Every current rocket journey to the Moon is one 30 cm step; interstellar travel is 160 km of those same steps.

Now think about speed. The fastest car on a highway covers 160 km in under 2 hours. But our fastest spacecraft (Voyager 1 at 17 km/s) covers that scale distance in the equivalent of 2,700 years on our model — not 2 hours. Reaching a meaningful fraction of light speed is not a matter of building a slightly better rocket; it requires a fundamentally different energy source. Chemical rockets burn fuel that releases ~10 MJ/kg; nuclear fission releases ~80,000 MJ/kg; matter-antimatter annihilation releases ~90,000,000 MJ/kg. The gap between current technology and what interstellar travel demands is not incremental — it is nine orders of magnitude in energy density.

The constraint is not imagination or engineering ambition. It is the physics of kinetic energy scaling with the square of velocity: to double your speed, you need four times the energy.

5. Real-world connection

India's space programme is actively expanding toward the outer solar system: Mangalyaan (Mars, 2014), Chandrayaan-3 (Moon, 2023), Aditya-L1 (Sun-Earth L1 point, 2023), Shukrayaan-1 (Venus, planned 2031), Gaganyaan (human spaceflight, planned 2026). Each mission applies the same orbital mechanics and energy calculations relevant to interstellar physics — Hohmann transfers, gravity assists, launch window optimisation.

ISRO's future plans include missions to asteroids. India's IISER and IIT research groups work on advanced propulsion concepts including photonic sails and fusion propulsion — fundamental research toward eventual deep-space capability. The Breakthrough Starshot project (backed by Yuri Milner, endorsed by the late Stephen Hawking) aims to launch laser-propelled gram-scale probes to Proxima Centauri within decades using a 100 GW ground-based laser array.

6. Mini activity — 5 minutes

Scale model of stellar distances: Use a 1 cm marble or coin as the Sun. At this scale, 1 astronomical unit (AU, Earth-Sun distance) = 1.1 m. Pace out 1.1 m — that is Earth's orbit.

Proxima Centauri at this scale is approximately 290 km away. Look at a map of your city and find a city 290 km from your home — that is how far away the nearest star is if the Sun is the size of a marble in your hand. The Andromeda Galaxy (our nearest large galactic neighbour) would be 7.6 million km away at this scale — about 20 times the actual Earth-Moon distance. The observable universe edge would be 430 billion km — roughly three times the distance from Earth to Pluto. Interstellar travel is mind-boggling not because of any single number but because every level of scale is incomprehensible compared to the previous one.

7. Think deeper

The Fermi Paradox: If interstellar travel is possible — even over millions of years at slow speeds — and the universe is 13.8 billion years old, why have we detected no sign of advanced civilisations? Proposed solutions:

  • Great Filter: Most civilisations destroy themselves (war, climate change, pandemic) before achieving interstellar capability. If the filter is ahead of us, that is the most frightening possibility.
  • Zoo hypothesis: We are deliberately kept isolated by civilisations that have already achieved interstellar travel.
  • Rare Earth: Complex multicellular life requires such specific conditions that it is extraordinarily rare.
  • Dark Forest (Liu Cixin's Three-Body Problem): Civilisations hide to avoid detection by hostile others.

The Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) scans radio waves. India's GMRT participates in searches for anomalous radio signals from stellar directions. The answer to the Fermi Paradox — if found — may be the most consequential discovery in human history.

Predict: Breakthrough Starshot's gram-scale probe reaches Proxima Centauri after 21 years and needs to transmit data back to Earth. The signal travels at the speed of light. How long after launch will scientists on Earth receive the first data from Proxima Centauri? If the probe discovers Proxima b (a potentially habitable planet) has a water ocean, what would be the minimum time before a follow-up mission could arrive, assuming the same 0.2c travel speed and a 10-year preparation window?

8. Quick quiz

Q1

What is the primary scientific challenge in travelling to another star system?

  • A. Stars are too hot to approach, so spacecraft would melt before reaching the destination
  • B. Interstellar space has too much gravity from dark matter, preventing spacecraft from moving outward
  • C. The vast distances to even the nearest stars mean travel times of thousands of years at current speeds

Correct answer: C

Q2

Why is understanding the scientific mechanism of interstellar travel challenges more useful than just saying it is hard?

  • A. Knowing it is hard is enough to conclude that it will never happen so no mechanism is needed
  • B. A mechanism explains cause-and-effect and allows prediction in new situations
  • C. Travel challenges are engineering problems not scientific ones, so mechanisms are irrelevant

Correct answer: B

Q3

If a spacecraft could travel at 10% of the speed of light, what would you predict about reaching Proxima Centauri?

  • A. The journey would take about one year because Proxima Centauri is only 4 light-minutes away
  • B. The journey would be instantaneous because at 10% light speed relativistic effects eliminate distance
  • C. The journey would take roughly 40 years because Proxima Centauri is about 4.2 light-years away

Correct answer: C

Challenge

A spacecraft of mass 1,000 kg is to be accelerated to 5% of the speed of light (0.05c) for an interstellar mission.

Calculate the kinetic energy required using KE = ½mv² (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s). The world's current total annual energy production is approximately 6 × 10²⁰ J. Express the required energy as a fraction of world annual production. Now consider: nuclear fusion fuel (deuterium) releases approximately 3 × 10¹⁴ J/kg. How many kilograms of fusion fuel would be needed? If the spacecraft's fuel mass cannot exceed 10× the payload mass (a practical engineering constraint called the mass ratio), is this mission feasible with fusion propulsion — and what does this tell you about why interstellar travel is genuinely difficult even with advanced technology?

10. One surprising idea

At speeds approaching the speed of light, time dilation means that interstellar travel is subjectively short for the traveller even when centuries pass on Earth. A spacecraft travelling at 99.9% of light speed would experience only 4.5 years during a journey that takes 283 years in Earth's reference frame. In principle, a person could travel to a galaxy 1,000 light-years away and age only a few decades — but return to find that 1,000 years had passed on Earth. Interstellar travel does not violate physics; it violates human social continuity. The traveller would return to a civilisation that had advanced 1,000 years beyond their departure point — everyone they knew would be dead, and they would be a stranger from the past.

Daily Takeaway

The nearest star is so far away that our fastest current spacecraft would take 74,800 years to reach it — interstellar travel is not impossible in principle, but the energy required dwarfs anything humanity has ever produced.
