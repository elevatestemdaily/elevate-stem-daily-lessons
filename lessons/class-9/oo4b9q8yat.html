Today's Science Mystery

How do chemical bonds hold matter together?

10 min readDaily STEMClass 9
Today's Mission

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds, explain why atoms bond at all, and predict what forms when a single-electron atom bonds with an atom needing one electron.

1. The mystery

Salt dissolves in water. Sand doesn't. Iron conducts electricity. Wood doesn't. Diamond is the hardest natural material. Graphite is slippery enough for pencil lead — yet both are pure carbon. All these differences come from how atoms are bonded together.

2. The science behind it

Why atoms bond: Atoms are most stable with full outer electron shells. Most reactions drive atoms toward a full shell (8 electrons for most elements — the octet rule).

Ionic bond: One atom gives electrons to another. Results in oppositely charged ions (cation +, anion −) attracted by electrostatic force. Example: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻; e⁻ + Cl → Cl⁻; Na⁺Cl⁻ = table salt. High melting point, conducts electricity when dissolved.

Covalent bond: Atoms share electrons. Both atoms benefit from the shared pair filling their shells. H₂: H-H (share 2 electrons). H₂O: O shares 1 pair with each H. Strong bonds, usually non-conductors, variable melting points.

Metallic bond: Metal atoms release outer electrons into a "sea" of delocalised electrons shared by all cations. Explains metals: electrical conductivity (free electrons carry charge), thermal conductivity, malleability (cations slide), ductility.

Bond energy: Energy required to break a bond. C–C: 347 kJ/mol. C=C: 614 kJ/mol. C≡C: 839 kJ/mol. Stronger bonds release more energy when formed.

3. Build the mental model

Think of chemical bonding as three different strategies for sharing resources in a community:

  • Ionic bonding is like a permanent transfer of ownership: one family gives their extra land to a neighbouring family that was short. Both families are now more stable (full outer shells), but the transfer is done — the first family is now positively charged (gave something away), the second is negatively charged (received something). They are attracted to each other and stay near each other. This is sodium chloride: sodium gave an electron to chlorine.

  • Covalent bonding is like a shared well between two neighbours: neither owns it fully, but both draw water from it. The sharing arrangement benefits both. Hydrogen and oxygen share electrons in water; neither atom fully owns the shared pair, but both achieve a stable outer shell.

  • Metallic bonding is like a communal electricity grid in a colony: every house (metal cation) plugs in, but the electrons flow freely through the entire grid rather than belonging to any one house. This is why metals conduct: the free-flowing electron "sea" carries charge wherever it is needed.

The octet rule is the underlying driver: atoms want 8 electrons in their outer shell (or 2 for hydrogen and helium). Every bonding strategy is a different way to achieve that goal.

5. Real-world connection

India's chemical industry (₹9 lakh crore/year, world's 3rd largest) is built on bond chemistry. Fertilisers (IFFCO, Rashtriya Chemicals): ionic bonds in ammonium nitrate (NH₄NO₃). Plastics (Reliance Industries, world's 4th largest polyester producer): covalent bonds in polymer chains. Steel (TATA, JSW): metallic bonds in iron and carbon alloys. Cement: ionic and covalent bonds in calcium silicate hydrate give it strength.

The difference between diamond and graphite — both pure carbon — is entirely about how the covalent bonds are arranged in three dimensions. Diamond has each carbon bonded to four others in a rigid tetrahedral lattice (Mohs hardness 10). Graphite has each carbon bonded to only three others in flat sheets, with weak forces between sheets (slippery, used in pencil lead and as a lubricant in high-temperature machinery). Indian diamond cutting and polishing in Surat, Gujarat, employs over 600,000 workers and processes 90% of the world's rough diamonds — an industry built entirely on understanding the strength of covalent carbon bonds.

6. Mini activity — 5 minutes

Dissolve NaCl and sugar in separate cups of water. Connect a battery to two carbon electrodes in each cup via a light bulb. NaCl solution lights the bulb (ions conduct electricity). Sugar solution doesn't (covalent molecules, no free ions). You've tested ionic vs covalent bonding with a battery and a bulb.

Extend the test: try distilled water alone (no ions, bulb stays dark), then add a pinch of NaCl gradually and watch the bulb brighten as ion concentration increases. This shows that electrical conductivity in solution is directly proportional to ion concentration — a consequence of ionic bonding and the release of mobile charged particles on dissolution.

7. Think deeper

Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon (Z=6) but have radically different properties because of bond arrangement. Diamond: each carbon bonds covalently to 4 others in a tetrahedral 3D lattice — every bond must break to deform it → hardest known material (Mohs 10). Graphite: each carbon bonds to 3 others in flat hexagonal sheets — strong within layers, weak between layers → slippery, good lubricant, conducts electricity within layers. Graphene (single layer of graphite) is the strongest material by weight ever measured — 100× stronger than steel — discovered 2004, Nobel Prize 2010.

Predict: Graphene conducts electricity within its plane but not perpendicular to it. Using the metallic bonding model (delocalised electrons free to move), explain why graphene's delocalised electrons — which move within the hexagonal carbon sheet — would allow in-plane conduction but resist out-of-plane conduction. How does this directional property differ from regular metallic conductors like copper, where electrons flow in all directions?

8. Quick quiz

Q1

What is the correct description of how ionic bonds form?

  • A. Ionic bonds form when two atoms share electrons equally in overlapping outer orbitals
  • B. Ionic bonds form when atoms merge their nuclei together under high temperature and pressure
  • C. Ionic bonds form when one atom transfers electrons to another, creating attracting oppositely charged ions

Correct answer: C

Q2

Why is knowing the mechanism of chemical bonding more useful than memorising bond definitions?

  • A. Bond definitions are too long to memorise, so mechanisms replace them in advanced study
  • B. A mechanism explains cause-and-effect and allows prediction in new situations
  • C. Mechanisms are shorter and therefore easier to write in limited examination time

Correct answer: B

Q3

If an atom with one outer electron bonded with an atom that needed one electron, what would form?

  • A. An ionic compound where the electron transfers, forming a positive and negative ion pair
  • B. A covalent compound where both atoms share their electrons equally across all shells
  • C. A metallic lattice because single-electron atoms always form metallic structures

Correct answer: A

Challenge

Magnesium oxide (MgO) and sodium chloride (NaCl) are both ionic compounds, but MgO has a melting point of 2,852°C while NaCl melts at only 801°C. Both are ionic, so why the enormous difference?

Use your knowledge of ionic bonding (electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions) to explain this. Consider the charge of the ions involved: Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ vs Na⁺ and Cl⁻. Coulomb's law states that electrostatic force is proportional to the product of the charges. Calculate the relative attraction strength (as a ratio) for MgO vs NaCl, and use this to explain the melting point difference. Conclude with a prediction: would calcium oxide (CaO, Ca²⁺ and O²⁻) have a melting point closer to NaCl or MgO?

10. One surprising idea

The reason you cannot walk through a wall is not because matter is solid — it is because of electromagnetic repulsion between electron clouds. Atoms are 99.999% empty space. When you press your hand against a table, no actual matter is touching; the electron clouds of your hand and the table repel each other electromagnetically and prevent the nuclei from getting close. The "solidity" of matter is entirely an illusion created by the electromagnetic force operating between the bonded electron clouds on surfaces. The wall is mostly vacuum — and so are you.

Daily Takeaway

Atoms bond to complete their outer electron shells — ionic bonds transfer electrons to form charged ions, covalent bonds share electrons between atoms, and metallic bonds release electrons into a shared sea — and the type of bond determines every physical property of the substance.
